7. What we could not test

  • We re-ran the evaluation code in the case folder, not the authors' original pipeline, raw data or software versions.
  • Only two evaluation steps (factors A and B) were varied per run. Other leakage types were not examined, and the model, metric and features are held fixed by construction.
  • Intervals come from an unpaired bootstrap over test rows for 3 seeds (42/43/44). They do not cover variation across training sets, model initialisations or a fresh draw of the data.
  • Contamination: the model that proposed the fix recognised the source of this case in the memorisation probe (full_agent_view), so its choice of what to fix may be recalled rather than discovered. The measured numbers come from the sandbox, not from the model.
  • Small scored sample (10 rows after the change): intervals are wide.

9. Suggested note to the authors

The headline result (3%) appears to depend on the evaluation protocol: feature selection sees the rows that are later scored.

In our reanalysis the code was run with only claimed_eval changed (fidelity guard: only step(s) claimed_eval changed (fit_scope/split)). The same model then scored 46.7% against 99.3% under the claimed protocol (metric: accuracy), a drop of +52.7 pp (mean of seeds 42/43/44; 95% unpaired-bootstrap intervals for the drop: seed 42 [+30.0, +90.0]; seed 43 [+28.0, +88.0]; seed 44 [+10.0, +70.0]; 50 rows scored by the claim, 10 after the change).

This matches the leakage pattern 'Feature selection jointly on training and test data' (L1.3, Kapoor and Narayanan 2023). We would ask the authors to report the score under repeating feature selection inside each training fold only, or to explain why the current protocol is appropriate for the stated use.

We did not re-run the authors' original pipeline and cannot rule out that other choices explain part of the gap.
