I built NullLab for machine-learning authors investigating an evaluation before trusting it. The product starts with your question and your materials, in a persistent research workdesk.

Attach a CSV, your compatible Python pipeline, and notes. NullLab preserves the original files, inspects their structure without executing the upload, and helps identify the information needed for a supported study.

Confirm the target, intended decision, prediction time and population. Quoted answers become reviewable drafts. Choose your supplied pipeline or a clearly labelled generated baseline. Review the concrete plan before starting. Missing facts and unsupported designs remain explicit.

In Meta mode, Muse Spark proposes a typed control. Optional Muse Wonder independently challenges the methodology and interpretation. Local guards check the proposed change, preserve the classifier, and execute the experiment. This diagram explains those handoffs; it is not live activity.

Each run keeps exact inputs, tested controls, predictions, environment and attempted calls. Export the verified evidence, or an explicitly unmeasured assessment. Changed inputs cannot inherit an old result. A clean twin with no measured control says not tested.

Local rules and execution need no API key. Meta reasoning is optional and acknowledged. Our internal evaluations retain failures; broad accuracy and outside-user impact remain unproven. The supported scope is binary tabular evaluation.
